## 1. My Desired Identity

### Entrepreneur / Startup CEO

- [ ]  I worked on building or validating a product or business idea.
- [ ]  I took at least one step toward turning an idea into a real product.

### Freelancer

- [ ]  I improved a skill that has real market value.
- [ ]  I searched for suitable opportunities or contacted potential clients.
- [ ]  I delivered work or made measurable progress toward getting paid.

### Social Person / Creator

- [ ]  I initiated a conversation instead of always waiting for others.
- [ ]  I participated meaningfully in an online community such as Reddit.
- [ ]  WEAKLY I created, shared, or developed an idea for YouTube content.
- [ ]  I attended a social event or took a step toward meeting new people.

### Avid Reader

- [ ]  I read a book for enjoyment, not just to finish it.
- [ ]  I explored history, technology news, or another subject out of curiosity.
- [ ]  I made reading an easy, natural part of my day.
- [ ]  I read because I wanted to, not merely to check a box.

### Disciplined Person

- [ ]  I identified my most important commitments before starting my day.
- [ ]  I did what I had committed to doing, even when I lacked motivation.
- [ ]  I started an important task at the time I had planned.
- [ ]  When I missed a habit, I returned to it instead of abandoning the system.

### Balanced Person

- [ ]  I enjoyed gaming, movies, series, anime, or other leisure without neglecting important responsibilities.
- [ ]  I stopped or took a break when entertainment began interfering with my priorities.
- [ ]  I allowed myself to rest without feeling that every moment must be productive.
- [ ]  I protected my sleep and basic needs instead of sacrificing them for work.

## 2. Atomic Habits: Am I Following the Book?

### Law 1 — Make It Obvious

- [ ]  I defined when and where I would perform my important habits.
- [ ]  I prepared my environment to make good habits visible.
- [ ]  I identified the cues that trigger my unwanted behaviors.
- [ ]  I used habit stacking where appropriate: after [existing habit], I will [new habit].

### Law 2 — Make It Attractive

- [ ]  I connected at least one useful habit with something I enjoy.
- [ ]  I chose learning and reading material that genuinely interests me.
- [ ]  I spent time around people or communities that reinforce the identity I want.

### Law 3 — Make It Easy

- [ ]  I reduced friction for my most important habits.
- [ ]  I used a small starting action when a task felt difficult.
- [ ]  I applied the Two-Minute Rule when starting or establishing a new habit.
- [ ]  I made distracting behaviors less convenient.

### Law 4 — Make It Satisfying

- [ ]  I tracked my habits or recorded evidence of progress.
- [ ]  I acknowledged small wins instead of waiting for major results.
- [ ]  I used a reward or immediate positive feedback where appropriate.
- [ ]  I avoided letting one missed day become a repeated pattern.

### Identity-Based Habits

- [ ]  I defined the type of person I want to become, not only the outcomes I want.
- [ ]  I collected real evidence for my desired identity through actions.
- [ ]  I judged progress by repeated behavior, not by intentions or self-labels.

Identity Building OS

- [ ]  I treated each action as a vote, not as proof that I must be perfect.

## 3. My Anti-Identity: What I Choose Not to Become

- [ ]  I did not allow gaming to displace essential responsibilities.
- [ ]  I avoided TikTok and other brainrot-oriented feeds.
- [ ]  I avoided mindless scrolling when I had more important intentions.
- [ ]  I did not confuse being busy with making meaningful progress.
- [ ]  I did not use discipline as an excuse to eliminate all enjoyment.
- [ ]  I noticed when entertainment was becoming compulsive and adjusted my environment.

**Principle:** I am not trying to eliminate fun. I am choosing how fun fits into my life.

- [ ]  **Becoming consistent:** These behaviors are increasingly natural and resilient to changes in motivation.
- [ ]  **Maintaining identity:** I sustain the behaviors while adapting to changing circumstances.

## Rules for Using This Checklist

1. Review weekly, not obsessively every day.
2. Check a box only when you have evidence that you actually did the behavior.
3. Do not expect every identity to receive equal attention every week.
4. Missing a habit is information, not a verdict on your character.
5. Use the Two-Minute Rule to make starting easier, not as a permanent substitute for meaningful work.
6. Prioritize repeated behavior over streaks, perfect scores, and self-image.
7. Keep entertainment as a legitimate part of a balanced life.
